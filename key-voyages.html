Bucareli era — the reconnaissance of the coast
1769

The Sacred Expedition — sea arm · Vila, Pérez, Callegan

Spain
ShipsSan Carlos, San Antonio, San José — paquebotes
ReachedSan Diego Bay
CommandersVicente Vila · Juan Pérez · "Callegan"

The seaborne half of the Gálvez–Portolá occupation. The San Antonio reached San Diego on 11 April 1769; the San Carlos arrived 29 April with its crew wrecked by scurvy. The third supply ship, the San José, sailed again from Cabo San Lucas in May 1770 and was lost with all hands — the expedition's great maritime failure (it never reached San Diego).

● In registry — cites added Vila diary, ed. Rose (1911); Costansó, Diario histórico, ed. Teggart (1911); Bancroft I
1774

Juan Pérez — first voyage up the coast · pilot E. J. Martínez

Spain
ShipSantiago (Nueva Galicia) — frigate
ReachedSan Diego · Monterey · north to ~54–55°N (lat. unverified)
AboardDiarists Peña & Crespí

The first Spanish exploration north of Monterey. Touched San Diego then Monterey (spring 1774), then ran up to ~54°40′–55°N off Haida Gwaii and made the first European contact at Nootka — four years before Cook. He made no landing or act of possession, the very failure that prompted the 1775 follow-up. Pilot Esteban José Martínez would later force the Nootka crisis.

● Now added to registry Beals, Juan Pérez on the NW Coast (OHS, 1989); Bolton, Fray Juan Crespí (1927)
1775

Heceta & Bodega y Quadra — the Bucareli survey

Spain
ShipsSantiago (Heceta) · Sonora (Bodega) — frigate & schooner
ReachedTrinidad Bay · Bodega Bay · Monterey
RangeHeceta to ~49°N · Bodega to ~58°N

A possession-taking survey against feared Russian encroachment. Anchored Trinidad Bay 9–19 June (first contact with the Yurok at Tsurai; a possession ceremony on Trinidad Head). Bodega's Sonora discovered and charted Bodega Bay; both ships reunited at Monterey on 7 October 1775.

● In registry Beals, For Honor and Country: Diary of Bruno de Hezeta (1985); Sierra diary, ed. Wagner (CHSQ 9:3, 1930)
1775

Juan Manuel de Ayala — first ship into San Francisco Bay · pilot Cañizares

Spain
ShipSan Carlos — paquebot
ReachedSan Francisco Bay (interior)
AboardPilots Cañizares & Aguirre · chaplain Santa María

The first vessel ever to enter San Francisco Bay. While Ayala lay at Angel Island (named on this voyage), pilot José de Cañizares carried out the first boat survey of the bay across a six-week stay. Entry date: 5 Aug 1775 per the primary Santa María diary (and Calif. Landmark 236); Bancroft's night-of-1-Aug was a pre-diary reconstruction, now superseded.

● In registry — cite upgraded Santa María diary, ed. Galvin, First Spanish Entry into SF Bay, 1775 (1971)
1779

Arteaga & Bodega y Quadra — the Alaska voyage

Spain
ShipsPrincesa · Favorita — frigates
ReachedSan Francisco, 14/15 Sept – 30 Oct 1779 · north to 61°17′N (Alaska)
CommandingIgnacio de Arteaga; Bodega 2nd

The third Bucareli-era reconnaissance of the far north — Spain's highest latitude ever, 61°17′N in Prince William Sound (23 July 1779), with possession ceremonies. On the return the scurvy-ridden crews recuperated six weeks at San Francisco (the Favorita entered 14 Sept, the Princesa the next day), cut short by news of Bucareli's death and war with England.

● Port resolved & in registry "unlocated" → San Francisco Bancroft I:329; Maurelle, Relación de 1779 (MS); Tovell (2008)
The foreigners arrive & the Nootka years
1786

La Pérouse — the first foreign visit · with de Langle

France
ShipsBoussole · Astrolabe — frigates
ReachedMonterey, 14–24 September 1786
HostGov. Pedro Fages

The first foreign expedition to touch Spanish California. Ten days at Monterey yielded the era's sharpest outside ethnography of the mission-Indian system, along with notes on agriculture and the nascent sea-otter trade — a scientific circumnavigation that would vanish in the Pacific two years later.

● In registry La Pérouse, Voyage Round the World (1799), vol. II chs. X–XII; Dunmore ed., Hakluyt 2nd ser. 179–180 (1994–95)
1788

Martínez & López de Haro — the Russian reconnaissance

Spain
ShipsPrincesa (Martínez) · San Carlos (López de Haro)
ReachedMonterey, 17 Sept – 14 Oct 1788 (Princesa only)
PurposeScouting Russian activity in Alaska

A reconnaissance of the Russian advance on the Alaska coast (Unalaska; met Zaikov) — the prelude to Martínez's seizure of British vessels at Nootka in 1789 that ignited the Nootka Crisis. The Princesa paused at Monterey 17 Sept–14 Oct 1788; the San Carlos ran straight to San Blas. Distinct from Martínez's 1779 and 1789 voyages — don't conflate.

● In registry — cites added Bancroft I:444; AGI ESTADO 43 N.12; Cook, Flood Tide of Empire (Yale, 1973)
1790

Fidalgo & Quimper — Nootka-return touch

Spain
ShipsSan Carlos (Fidalgo) · Princesa Real (Quimper)
ReachedMonterey, September 1790
CommandersSalvador Fidalgo · Manuel Quimper

A dispatch-and-supply call at Monterey on the return from the 1790 northern reconnaissance out of Nootka — Fidalgo to Prince William Sound / Cook Inlet, Quimper charting the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Quimper's ship was the Princesa Real (the captured British Princess Royal), not the frigate Princesa.

● In registry — cites added Wagner, Spanish Explorations in the Strait of Juan de Fuca (1933); Bancroft I:444–45
The Malaspina & Vancouver moment — science and diplomacy
1791

Malaspina & Bustamante — the grand scientific expedition

Spain
ShipsDescubierta (Malaspina) · Atrevida (Bustamante) — corvettes
ReachedMonterey, 13–25 September 1791; coastal survey to Cabo San Lucas
AboardBauzá, Espinosa, Galiano, Valdés; naturalist Haenke; artist Suría (Née & Guío stayed in Mexico)

Spain's answer to Cook and La Pérouse — the most ambitious scientific voyage the empire ever sent to the Pacific. Twelve days at Monterey of botanical, astronomical, and ethnographic work with Fr. Lasuén's help, then a survey run down the coast past San Francisco and Cape Mendocino to Cabo San Lucas. Bancroft's roster stars Née and Guío as having "remained behind in Mexico" — so the naturalist and artist actually at Monterey were Haenke and Suría.

● Now added to registry arrival 13 Sept (Bancroft) vs 11 Sept (Bustamante's account) Cutter, Malaspina in California (1960); Novo y Colson ed. (1885); Hakluyt ed. vol. II (2003)
1792

Galiano & Valdés — the Sutil and Mexicana

Spain
ShipsSutil (Galiano) · Mexicana (Valdés) — schooners
ReachedMonterey, 22 Sept – 26 Oct 1792
MissionSurvey of the Strait of Juan de Fuca

The last Spanish exploration of the Northwest Coast: detached from the Malaspina enterprise, the two schooners charted the Strait of Juan de Fuca and circumnavigated Vancouver Island alongside Vancouver, then spent five weeks at Monterey writing up the northern charts. Published as the famous Relación del viage… Sutil y Mexicana (Madrid, 1802). The C-A 14 record dated Dec 1793 is a garbled duplicate of this 1792 touch.

● In registry — dates fixed Relación… Sutil y Mexicana (1802); Kendrick ed. (1991); Bancroft I:506–07
1792–93

Bodega y Quadra — the Monterey winter · Nootka commissioner

Spain
Shipfrigate Activa (his broad pennant)
ReachedMonterey, 9 Oct 1792 – sailed 14/15 Jan 1793
BusinessNootka Convention diplomacy with Vancouver

After the inconclusive Nootka sovereignty talks, the Spanish commissioner wintered at Monterey. The celebrated cordiality between Bodega and Vancouver — dinners, gifts, exchanged charts — played out partly here, even as the two empires contested the coast. Ship resolved: his flagship was the Activa; the Saturnina is a separate survey schooner (the guns Bodega offered San Francisco were to come on it).

● Ship resolved & in registry Bancroft I:509–13; Bodega 1792 journal (Tovell); C-A 54 i.119
1792–94

George Vancouver — three visits

Britain
ShipsDiscovery · Chatham · storeship Daedalus
ReachedSan Francisco · Monterey · Santa Barbara · San Diego
HostsSal, Arrillaga, Borica, Bodega

The first foreign warship into San Francisco Bay (14 Nov 1792) and the wintering at Monterey opened three years of British presence — Nootka diplomacy fused with the great Pacific survey. The visits alarmed Madrid and triggered the 1793 assessment to fortify San Francisco, Monterey, and San Diego and to occupy Bodega. The second visit (1793) met a markedly cooler reception.

● In registry Vancouver, Voyage of Discovery (1798), ed. Lamb, Hakluyt 2nd ser. 163–166 (1984); Menzies journal, ed. Eastwood (CHSQ 2:4, 1924); C-A 6, 54
1793

Martínez y Zayas — the Trinidad Bay survey

Spain
Shipgoleta Mexicana (Eliza overall on the Activa)
ReachedTrinidad Bay · Bodega Bay
WorkDetailed re-survey & charting

The 1793 Eliza reconnaissance charted the coast from Juan de Fuca to San Francisco to scout a settlement site against British and Russian encroachment. Martínez y Zayas commanded the consort Mexicana (the same hull from the 1792 Galiano voyage) and did the inshore charting of Trinidad Bay; his chart survives in Wagner. Vessel resolved: the Mexicana, previously logged as "unnamed."

● Vessel resolved & in registry Wagner, Cartography of the NW Coast (1937); Sierra/Wagner (1930), chart p. 219
The monopoly breaks — foreign traders
1796Jun

W. R. Broughton — HMS Providence

Britain
ShipHMS Providence — sloop-of-war
ReachedSan Francisco ~10 June · Monterey, June 1796
NoteBoats on the Salinas were fired upon

Broughton — formerly of Vancouver's Chatham — returned on his own Pacific survey, entering with an 11-gun salute and resupplying (a ~$308 bill survives). Corrected date: the Providence was here in June 1796 (departed 18 June–8 July), not October — the October date belongs to the Otter, with which it had been conflated.

● In registry — date corrected Broughton, Voyage to the N. Pacific (1804); Bancroft I:538–39 (St. Pap. Sac. vi.85–6)
1796

Ebenezer Dorr — the Otter, first U.S. ship

U.S.
ShipOtter of Boston — 6 guns, 26 men
ReachedMonterey, 29 Oct – 6 Nov 1796
TradeSea otter

The first vessel from the United States to anchor in a Californian port — and by night it forced ashore a dozen Port Jackson (Botany Bay) convict-stowaways, set to work as carpenters and smiths at 19¢/day. A sea-otter voyage that opened the American maritime fur presence on the coast, the commercial future the Spanish system could not keep out.

● In registry Bancroft I:538–40 (Borica corr., St. Pap. Sac. iv, vi); no published Dorr narrative
The fur rush & the Russian approach (1806–1818)
1806

Nikolai Rezanov — the Juno · capt. Khvostov

Russia
ShipJuno — ex-American, bought at Sitka
ReachedSan Francisco, ~April – 21 May 1806
AboardRezanov (RAC director); naturalist Langsdorff

A relief voyage to save the starving Russian-American Company colony at Sitka. Rezanov ran the Juno past the presidio guns and spent six weeks trading its cargo for grain with comandante Argüello and Gov. Arrillaga — a deal greased by his betrothal to the young Concepción Argüello. He sailed with a full hold, then died crossing Siberia in 1807; the romance became legend. Dates calendar-dependent (Old Style vs New): arrival ~5 Apr (Spanish) / 8 Apr NS.

● In registry Langsdorff, Voyages and Travels (1813–14), vol. II; Pierce ed., Rezanov Reconnoiters California, 1806; AVPRI
1808–09

Ivan Kuskov — the Kodiak · the Fort Ross prelude

Russia
ShipKodiak — RAC (capt. Petrov)
ReachedBodega Bay, 28 Dec 1808 – 18 Aug 1809
Party130 hunters; 2,350 otter taken

Kuskov wintered the RAC hunting brig at Bodega — the reconnaissance that chose the ground for the Russian colony. The Spanish knew only of "a Russian frigate anchored at Bodega" (from deserters). Three years later this became Fort Ross, the Russian foothold that reshaped the north coast. Identified only by joining the Spanish and Russian archives.

● In registry Ogden 1941; Bolkhovitinov / Gibson–Istomin; C-A 12 d324 (the Spanish intelligence)
1816

Otto von Kotzebue — the Rurik

Russia
ShipRurik — brig (Rumyantsev expedition)
ReachedSan Francisco, 2 Oct – 1 Nov 1816
AboardChamisso, Eschscholtz, artist Choris

A Russian scientific circumnavigation. Its naturalists made the first systematic survey of San Francisco Bay's flora and fauna — Chamisso would name the California poppy for Eschscholtz — while Choris drew the earliest images of the presidio, missions, and Ohlone people. The party also exchanged word with the RAC at Fort Ross.

● In registry Kotzebue, A Voyage of Discovery (Longman, 1821), vol. I; Choris, Voyage pittoresque (1820–22)
1818Nov

Hipólito Bouchard — the privateer raid

Río de la Plata
ShipsLa Argentina (38–44 guns) · Santa Rosa
StruckMonterey · Refugio · Santa Barbara · San Juan Capistrano
Aboard2nd of the Santa Rosa = Peter Corney

The only foreign armed attack on Spanish California. Bouchard's ~600-man insurgent force overwhelmed Monterey's ~40 defenders on 22 Nov, occupied the capital for five days, and burned the presidio and fort; he then raided down the coast before leaving. Militarily marginal — Gov. Solá rebuilt Monterey by spring — but the province's baptism of fire, under the flag of the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata. A privateer with letters of marque, not a pirate; several departure dates are genuinely in conflict.

● Now added to registry Corney, Voyages in the Northern Pacific (1896); Bancroft II; Uhrowczik, The Burning of Monterey (2001)
1818Sep

Vasily Golovnin — the Kamchatka

Russia
ShipKamchatka — Russian navy sloop-of-war
ReachedMonterey & Bodega, September 1818
Aboardartist Mikhail Tikhanov

A Russian naval inspection voyage. Golovnin met RAC director Hagemeister at Monterey (Aleut hunters took otter "under the very noses of the Spaniards") and visited Bodega and Fort Ross. His narrative later advanced the Russian claim that there was no Spanish population north of San Francisco — a territorial argument, not a census. Date corrected: at Monterey in September; the 5 Oct 1818 in the registry was Solá's report date, not the port-call.

● In registry — date corrected Golovnin, Around the World on the Kamchatka, trans. Wiswell (1979); AGI Californias leg. 8
Survey & the eve of conquest (1826–1841)
1826–27

F. W. Beechey — HMS Blossom

Britain
ShipHMS Blossom — survey sloop, 16 guns
ReachedSan Francisco 6 Nov 1826; Monterey again 29 Oct 1827
Aboardnaturalist Collie; Lt. Edward Belcher

An Arctic-support voyage (to rendezvous with Franklin and Parry) that wintered twice in California. Beechey ran a careful hydrographic survey of San Francisco Bay and produced the most evenly accurate published observations of Mexican California to that date — and is nearly the sole source for Echeandía's partial-emancipation experiment.

● Now added to registry Beechey, Narrative of a Voyage to the Pacific and Beering's Strait (Colburn & Bentley, 1831), vol. II
1827–28

Auguste Duhaut-Cilly — le Héros

France
ShipLe Héros — French trader, 362 t
ReachedSF, Monterey, Sta Barbara, San Diego, Ross — Jan 1827 – Aug 1828
Aboardnaturalist Paolo Emilio Botta

A hide-and-tallow trading voyage that became the most geographically complete foreign eyewitness account of Mexican California — "no other navigator had visited so many of the Californian establishments" (Bancroft). The trade largely failed, but the resulting narrative, with Botta's ethnography and four lithographed views (incl. the Russian post at Bodega), is a first-rank source on pastoral society.

● In registry Duhaut-Cilly, Voyage autour du monde (1834–35); trans. Frugé & Harlow, A Voyage to California (UC Press, 1999)
1841

Charles Wilkes — the U.S. Exploring Expedition

U.S.
ShipsUSS Vincennes · Porpoise · Flying Fish · Oregon
ReachedSan Francisco Bay, 14 Aug – 1 Nov 1841
PartiesRinggold up the Sacramento; Emmons overland from Oregon

A politically pointed reconnaissance on the eve of American conquest. Wilkes's men charted San Francisco Bay, ran the boats up the Sacramento to ~39°N, and brought a party overland from Oregon. Wilkes openly discussed U.S. acquisition of the bay and keeping Britain out; his published assessment of California's weakness and value became an influential American document of 1841–46.

● Now added to registry Wilkes, Narrative of the U.S. Exploring Expedition (1844/1845), vol. V; Bancroft IV

Scope. Maritime only — ship-borne commanders and voyagers who reached California waters or ports, c. 1769–1841. This is a curated selection of the most consequential voyages, not a full census: behind them lie hundreds of supply, fur-trade, and hide-and-tallow calls (the full ship registry holds ~2,000). Land expeditions (Portolá overland, Anza, Fages, Font, Garcés) are deliberately excluded, as are the routine San Blas supply runs. The 19th-century selection favors the marquee scientific, naval, diplomatic, and raiding visits — the fur rush and the routine coasting trade are represented in the main registry table, not here.
